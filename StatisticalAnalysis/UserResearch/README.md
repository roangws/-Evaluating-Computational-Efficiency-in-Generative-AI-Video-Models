# User Perception Study — Generative AI Video Models

This study evaluates **user perception and preferences** across 5 generative AI video models through a comprehensive user research study with 32 participants.

---

## 📊 Study Overview

**Participants**: 32 (P001-P032)  
**Models Evaluated**: HunyuanVideo-1.5, Google Veo 3.1, CogVideoX-2b, CogVideoX-5b, CogVideoX1.5-5B  
**Evaluation Metrics**: 10 perception dimensions per model + preference rankings  
**Study Design**: Within-subjects (all participants rated all models)

| **Age Range** | 18-55 years (Mean: ~36 years) |
| **Gender** | Male (66%), Female (28%), Non-binary/Prefer not to say (6%) |

## 💡 Critical Insights

### 1. **User Preference vs. Technical Performance Divergence**

**Finding**: User preference rankings **do not align** with technical performance metrics (CLIP score, frame consistency).

- **User Preference Leaders**: Google Veo 3.1 (1.81) and HunyuanVideo-1.5 (1.94)
- **Technical Performance Leaders**: CogVideoX-5b (CLIP: 32.20) and CogVideoX1.5-5B (CLIP: 32.40)

**Implication**: Users prioritize **perceptual quality** (realism, visual appeal, ease of use) over algorithmic metrics. Technical benchmarks alone are insufficient for predicting user satisfaction.

### 2. **The "Ease of Use" Factor**

Google Veo 3.1 dominates in ease of use (4.66 ± 0.48), significantly higher than all local models (2.06-2.94). This suggests:
- **API-based solutions** have a strong UX advantage
- **Setup complexity** of local models negatively impacts user perception
- **Accessibility** is a major driver of overall satisfaction

### 3. **Realism Drives Preference**

HunyuanVideo-1.5 leads in realism (4.72 ± 0.46), which correlates with:
- High professional suitability (4.31)
- Strong overall satisfaction (4.25)
- Tied first-place preference ranking

**Takeaway**: Photorealistic output is critical for user acceptance, especially for professional use cases.

### 4. **Artifact Tolerance is Low**

CogVideoX1.5-5B has the highest artifact severity (3.66 ± 0.65), which correlates with:
- Lowest professional suitability (2.03)
- Lowest ease of use (2.06)
- Poor preference ranking (3.50)

**Conclusion**: Even minor visual artifacts significantly damage user perception and willingness to adopt.

### 5. **Willingness to Pay Aligns with Satisfaction**

| Model | Overall Satisfaction | Willingness to Pay | Correlation |
|-------|---------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Google Veo 3.1 | 4.38 | 3.91 | ✅ Strong |
| HunyuanVideo-1.5 | 4.25 | 3.31 | ✅ Strong |
| CogVideoX-2b | 2.81 | 2.12 | ✅ Strong |

Users are willing to pay more for models they perceive as higher quality, regardless of actual cost-efficiency.

### 6. **Technical Background Impact**

Participants with **AI/ML specialist** backgrounds (6% of sample) showed:
- Higher tolerance for artifacts
- Greater appreciation for technical complexity
- More balanced ratings across all models

**Implication**: Target audience expertise significantly influences perception and should inform product positioning.

## 🎯 Recommendations

### For Product Teams

1. **Prioritize Perceptual Quality Over Benchmarks**
   - Focus on realism and visual appeal, not just CLIP scores
   - Invest in artifact reduction (users are highly sensitive)

2. **Simplify Local Model Deployment**
   - Ease of use is a critical barrier (2.06-2.94 vs 4.66 for cloud)
   - Consider one-click installers or containerized solutions

3. **Market Segmentation Strategy**
   - **Professional users**: HunyuanVideo-1.5 (realism + professional suitability)
   - **Quick prototyping**: Google Veo 3.1 (speed + ease of use)
   - **Cost-conscious developers**: CogVideoX-2b (technical users with artifact tolerance)

### For Researchers

1. **Develop Perceptual Quality Metrics**
   - Current metrics (CLIP, frame consistency) don't predict user satisfaction
   - Need human-aligned evaluation frameworks

2. **Study Artifact Perception**
   - Understand which artifact types are most disruptive
   - Develop targeted mitigation strategies

3. **Investigate Realism Factors**
   - What specific visual features drive "realism" perception?
   - How to optimize for human perception vs. algorithmic metrics?

## 📊 Study Methodology

### Rating Scales

All perception metrics used **5-point Likert scales**:
- **1** = Very Poor/Strongly Disagree
- **2** = Poor/Disagree
- **3** = Neutral/Moderate
- **4** = Good/Agree
- **5** = Excellent/Strongly Agree

**Exception**: Artifacts Severity (lower is better)
- **1** = No artifacts
- **5** = Severe artifacts

### Preference Ranking

Participants ranked all 5 models from **1 (best) to 5 (worst)**:
- Mean rank calculated across all participants
- First-place votes counted separately
- Lower mean rank = better preference

### Statistical Analysis

- **Descriptive Statistics**: Mean, standard deviation, median
- **Sample Size**: n = 32 participants per model
- **Within-Subjects Design**: All participants evaluated all models (reduces individual variance)

---

## 🔍 Limitations

1. **Sample Size**: 32 participants (adequate for exploratory study, but larger sample recommended for generalization)
2. **Prompt Diversity**: Limited to 3 standardized prompts (may not capture full model capabilities)
3. **Self-Reported Metrics**: Subjective ratings may include response bias
4. **Technical Background Skew**: 47% technical professionals (may not represent general population)
5. **No Blind Testing**: Participants knew which model generated each video (potential brand bias)
